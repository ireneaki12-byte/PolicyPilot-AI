DATA BREACH POLICY AND PROCEDURES
7.1	Introduction
This policy document governs the acceptable usage of Kenya Aviation Limited Data Breach Policy and Procedures. This Data Breach Policy and Procedure outlines Kenya Aviation Limited’s approach to managing incidents involving the unauthorized access, loss, or disclosure of personal data. It is designed in alignment with global best practices, including ICAO guidelines, IATA data privacy standards, the Kenya Data Protection Act, and other applicable data protection regulations.

The policy provides a clear framework for identifying, reporting, assessing, and responding to data breaches. It ensures that all staff understand their roles and responsibilities in maintaining data integrity, and that the organization can respond swiftly and effectively to protect affected individuals and minimize business disruption.

By proactively addressing data breaches, Kenya Aviation Limited reaffirms its commitment to responsible data stewardship, operational excellence, and delivering a reliable experience with a passion for people. 

7.2	Objectives
The purpose of this policy is to establish a consistent and effective approach to identifying, reporting, managing, and investigating data breaches at Kenya Aviation Limited. It aims to:
i.	Mitigate potential harm caused by data breaches
ii.	Ensure timely compliance with regulatory requirements
iii.	Support our obligation to maintain public trust and safety as an aviation operator
iv.	Guide staff in the correct response during a suspected or confirmed data breach

7.3	Scope
To ensure a structured response to data breaches involving:

i.	Passenger Personally Identifiable Information (PII) (e.g., booking details, passport data).
ii.	Employee records.
iii.	Operational data (e.g., flight manifests, maintenance logs).
iv.	Financial transactions (e.g., payment card data).

This policy applies to all Kenya Aviation Limited employees (permanent, contract, interns), third-party service providers, vendors, and partners with access to Kenya Aviation Limited data and all data processed by or on behalf of Kenya Aviation Limited, across physical and digital platforms. It applies to digitally held data as well as data held in hard copy  
7.4	Data Security & Breach Requirements 
At Kenya Aviation Limited, a personal data breach is defined as any security incident—whether caused by a technical failure, lack of controls, or human error—that results in the unauthorized or accidental access, loss, alteration, destruction, or disclosure of personal data.
In the context of our airline operations, such breaches may involve passenger information, booking details, employee records, or operational system data. These incidents, hereafter referred to as “Data Incidents,” may constitute data breaches depending on their nature and impact.
Examples of data incidents that may qualify as personal data breaches include but are not limited to:
a)	Loss, theft, or misplacement of devices containing personal data, such as airline-issued laptops, mobile devices, or USB drives.
b)	Physical loss or theft of printed manifests, crew lists, or travel documents containing personal information.
c)	Misaddressed emails or boarding documentation sent to the wrong passengers or travel agents.
d)	Unauthorized access to passenger or employee data following a breach of access controls or credentials.
e)	External cyberattacks (e.g., via API endpoints or airline booking systems) intended to steal or compromise data.
f)	Natural disasters (e.g., fire or flood) affecting physical or digital data centers without sufficient data recovery procedures in place.
g)	Social engineering, phishing, or impersonation attacks that result in unauthorized data disclosure.
h)	Loss of access to personal data due to accidental deletion or corruption of files without a valid backup.
i)	Unauthorized modification of passenger or staff data, such as changes to flight details or employee records without approval.
Each suspected Data Incident must be assessed individually to determine whether it constitutes a data breach under applicable data protection laws. If a Data Incident does not involve Personal Data, it is not a Data Breach. Not all incidents involving personal data automatically qualify as reportable data breaches.
A Data Incident involving personal data may not qualify as a reportable breach if:
a)	The data is encrypted or anonymized in a manner that renders it unintelligible.
b)	The risk to individuals’ rights or freedoms is minimal or nonexistent.


6.4.1	Kenya Aviation Limited’s Data Protection Obligations
As a data controller and trusted airline brand, Kenya Aviation Limited is committed to maintaining robust technical and organizational measures to prevent, detect, and respond to data breaches. These commitments form part of our legal, operational, and brand integrity obligations.
Kenya Aviation Limited’s data protection infrastructure is supported by:
a)	A ‘Privacy by Design’ and ‘Privacy by Default’ approach integrated into systems such as NewSkies, Magenta Angani and partner APIs.
b)	Comprehensive Data Protection and Information Security Policies, reviewed regularly.
6.4.2	Data Security Measures in Place
To ensure a high level of protection, Kenya Aviation Limited has implemented the following controls:
a)	Pseudonymization and encryption of sensitive data across key platforms.
b)	Role-based access controls and system monitoring.
c)	Disaster Recovery and Business Continuity Plans (BCP) to ensure timely restoration of data and systems.
d)	Regular penetration testing, vulnerability assessments, and audit trails to evaluate the resilience of our IT infrastructure.
e)	Mandatory data protection training and awareness for all employees and contractors.
f)	Incident response simulations and breach drills for readiness across departments.
g)	Formal review and authorization procedures for data sharing, transfers, and disposal—monitored by the Data Protection Officer.
6.4.3	Objectives of this Policy Section
Kenya Aviation Limited’s objectives in implementing this data security and breach response framework are to:
•	Comply with the Kenya Data Protection Act, GDPR and international aviation data security standards.
•	Ensure effective procedures for identifying, investigating, reporting, and documenting data breaches.
•	Maintain a high standard of technical and organizational data security measures.
•	Leverage regular data audits and risk assessments to minimize exposure.
•	Ensure all breaches are reported to the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC) within 72 hours, where applicable.
•	Use the Data Breach Incident Log and Investigation Form to document all breaches and detect patterns.
•	Protect the privacy, safety, and trust of passengers, staff, and business partners.
•	Involve the Data Protection Officer in all breach investigations, risk mitigation, and follow-ups.
7.5	Data Breach Procedures & Guidelines – Kenya Aviation Limited
Kenya Aviation Limited has established robust objectives, systems, and controls to prevent, detect, and respond to personal data breaches. In the rare event a breach occurs, this procedure provides structured steps to ensure swift containment, investigation, and, where required, regulatory and stakeholder notification.
These procedures aim to mitigate risk, preserve the integrity of operations, protect affected individuals, and ensure compliance with applicable laws and aviation standards. This section provides a step-by-step approach for identifying, assessing, containing, and reporting a personal data breach. It also defines the timelines and actions necessary to comply with data protection regulations.
6.5.1	Breach Monitoring & Reporting
Kenya Aviation Limited has appointed a Data Protection Officer (DPO) who is responsible for reviewing, investigating, and managing all reported personal data breaches, regardless of severity.
All breaches—confirmed or suspected—must be reported to the DPO immediately upon discovery. No breach is considered too minor to be logged. Every incident is expectsed to be  reviewed and recorded, with follow-up actions tracked to ensure gaps are identified and closed.
Where breaches stem from system or process failures, updates are made to processes to prevent recurrence.
6.5.2	Breach Incidence Procedures

7.5.2.1	Identification and Reporting
•	Any individual suspecting or detecting a data breach must immediately notify the DPO via dataprotectionoffice@Kenya Aviation Limited.com and to the direct line manager.
•	The report should include:
o	Date and time of discovery
o	Nature and scope of the breach
o	Personal data involved
o	Potential risks and impact identified
o	Actions taken to contain and remediate the breach (if any)
•	A Breach Incident Form must be completed for every event noted. The DPO will be responsible for overseeing the completion of this form once containment is achieved and coordinating a full investigation and impact assessment.
7.5.2.2	Containment and Assessment
•	The DPO, IT, and security teams will assess the breach to determine its severity and impact.
•	Immediate actions will be taken to contain the breach, such as revoking access, isolating affected systems, or retrieving mistakenly shared data. A full investigation will be conducted by the DPO in collaboration with relevant teams (e.g. IT, HR, Legal, Commercial). Outcomes, actions taken, and key learnings will be shared with affected departments and senior management.
•	In cases where personal data has been compromised, Kenya Aviation Limited will notify regulators and affected individuals in accordance with this policy.
7.5.2.3	Risk Evaluation and Impact Assessment
•	The breach will be analyzed to determine the cause of the breach and the risk level to individuals and the organization.
•	Factors considered include the type of data compromised, potential harm, and the likelihood of misuse.
7.5.2.3.1	Human Error
Where human error is involved (e.g. sending passenger data to the wrong recipient), the DPO will:
•	Conduct root cause analysis
•	Review relevant procedures and training gaps
•	Recommend corrective actions (e.g. system restrictions, re-training)
Formal interviews and awareness sessions may be conducted to prevent recurrence.
7.5.2.3.2	System Error
Where system failures cause or contribute to a breach (e.g. misconfigured access permissions, downtime of secure platforms), the Information Systems team will:
•	Conduct a root cause analysis in collaboration with the DPO
•	Review system architecture and controls
•	Document findings and update technical safeguards
Immediate mitigation measures may include:
•	Password resets
•	Account suspensions
•	Restoration of data from backups
•	Enhanced monitoring of affected systems
7.5.2.3.3	Impact Assessment
The DPO will assess:
a)	The type and sensitivity of personal data involved
b)	The number of affected individuals
c)	Whether individuals are easily identifiable
d)	Security measures in place (e.g. encryption)
e)	Location and fate of the data (e.g. accessed, leaked, deleted)
f)	The likelihood and severity of harm (identity theft, fraud, reputational damage)
7.5.2.3.4	Risk Assessment Criteria
Each breach will be assigned a risk level (No Risk / Low / High), and mitigation actions will be logged accordingly. The severity and risk level of the data breach will be assigned based on the following criteria:
•	Type and sensitivity of data involved (e.g., passport numbers, booking history, payment data)
•	Number of affected data subjects (passengers, employees, etc.)
•	Exposure duration and access controls in place
•	Potential impact on Kenya Aviation Limited’s reputation, regulatory compliance, and operational continuity
6.5.3	Notification and Reporting
•	If the breach poses a significant risk, Kenya Aviation Limited will notify affected individuals promptly using the format captured in APPENDIX A.
•	The breach will be reported to the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach.This will be done using the format captured in APPENDIX B.
•	Internal stakeholders, including senior management and legal teams, will be informed.
6.5.4	Mitigation and Remediation
•	Corrective measures will be implemented to prevent future breaches, including system updates, security training, and policy revisions.
•	A post-breach review will be conducted to assess response effectiveness and enhance data protection measures.
7.6	Record Keeping
Maintaining comprehensive records of data breaches is essential for accountability and compliance. This section details the documentation requirements, including the nature of the breach and timeline, risk assessment and actions taken, communication to authorities and affected parties and lessons learned and corrective action taken; to ensure continuous improvement in Kenya Aviation Limited’s data protection practices.
Kenya Aviation Limited will maintain records of all data breaches for at least 6 years. These records will be used to improve security practices and ensure compliance with legal obligations.
7.7	Prevention, Training and Awareness
Regular training and awareness programs will be conducted to create awareness among employees on data protection, data protection best practices, breach response protocols, and their responsibilities in safeguarding personal data breach identification.
To prevent breaches, Kenya Aviation Limited will:
a)	Conduct annual data protection and cyber awareness training for all employees
b)	Regularly audit systems (e.g., NewSkies, GoNow, Magenta Angani, mobile apps)
c)	Implement access control, encryption, pseudonymization, and secure backups
d)	Maintain incident response playbooks and simulation drills
7.8	Review and Updates
This procedure will be reviewed periodically to ensure alignment with evolving data protection laws and industry best practices. Updates will be communicated to all relevant stakeholders.
